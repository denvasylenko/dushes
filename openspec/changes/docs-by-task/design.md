# Design

## Results guides: the facts (step 2b, 14.11)

Sources: Owner (marked [M] measured, [C] read from the code, [J] judgement, shown on the page as advice) and QA Lead's notes (qa-for-docs-get-the-result.md: host-controlled causes with example scenarios and fixes, the smallest scenario and the bar, site-agent routing notes; round 1 live on 2026-11-14 with agent, user and judge all Claude Haiku 5.5; a before/after set the same day with a Sonnet 5.5 judge, 7.36 to 7.96). Every score on a page comes from a data file with its round, date and models, never typed.

Instructions. UI agent prompt order [C]: persona, core rules (priorities: integrity, the form now, the business, defaults), the business's instructions (flow hints as advice, rules the agent may quote, business documents by name with a summary, skills by name and description). A business instruction wins over a default while integrity holds; one the form cannot follow now is dropped. Site agent [C]: the site map's `instructions` and the host's `context`; priorities: truth, the site as it is, the business's instructions, defaults. Do not repeat [C]: tone basics, never invent, the card is the confirmation, ask before submit or pay, do not name tools, say each thing once, no arithmetic, never send the user to another assistant. Leave out [M]: a rule that contradicts a tool or routing (the cabinet's "explain other pages' data yourself" fought hand_back); business examples in generic places. Current figure: round 1 (evals-20261114-145402.json, 14 Nov 14:54, agent, user and judge Claude Haiku 5.5) gives the held-out group 8.08; quote it with that date and those models. The before/after for this point is a different, smaller set: 6 held-out insurance scenarios, 7.42 to 8.75 after neutral examples (evals-20261114-032821.json and -035504.json, 14 Nov 03:28 and 03:55, models not recorded); shown only with that label and never compared with 8.08. Good example [J]: a 1–3 sentence persona and a flow hint that states structure the agent cannot see. Length [J]: rules as short checkable facts, one per line, about 20 at most; longer material goes to business documents.

Domain knowledge [C]: pack rules and glossary are always in the prompt (what most turns need, what a field means); business documents are searched on demand and cited. Field notes are the glossary: about the first 110 characters on the field's outline line, in full on the field card; useful [J]: where to find the value, its format, what it affects. Site map: path, title, description, keywords, tabs and sections; the page list is in the site agent's prompt, sections are search passages, keywords also route on form pages and match whole words only, so give each language's forms and stems [M]. Gap [M]: the UI agent does not read page sections yet (round 1 lost about 9 scenarios to a fee shown on the page but not in the pack); until page context lands, put a page's key facts in the pack's rules too.

Skills and tools [C]: only name and description are in the prompt; the description says when (the task and the user's words), not what the skill is. `forms:` scopes it (default all). Keeping two apart [J, then the overlapping-skills recipe's numbers]: different trigger nouns, one line on what each does not do (QA: "Not for X" lines held 10 of 10 in the recipe runs), no shared tool names. Host tools: one sentence on when to call, typed parameters, a short factual result.

Defaults to leave alone [C]: stay_on_forms true, prompt_cache auto, memory 30–60k tokens and 4 turns, max_tool_rounds 8–10, confirm_tools for anything that submits, pays or cancels, suggestions max 3, learn_dependencies true, effort low for chat [J/M]; a thread's tool list stays stable across turns [M].

Check your setup: QA's smallest scenario YAML, run with a fixed judge; the bar: held-out average at least 8.5 and no scenario below 6; a failed never_says caps the score at 3.
