The owner's task (through the CPO): how the idea of the "goal snapshot" and ideas close to it in meaning are already done in AI, and what of it to integrate. For each direction: what it is, what works and what does not (with figures), what exactly we take into the module, the code or Jev, and the size. The figures are taken from abstracts and reviews; the full texts were not opened; figures from different works cannot be compared with each other. The module itself and the anatomy are on [The goal module](../../how-it-works/goal-module/), the structure on [The goal snapshot](../goal-snapshot/).

**The main point.** In every mature direction the conclusion is the same: a person's goal has to be kept **as an explicit structure outside the model** and **put into the context whole every turn**. One cannot hope that the model will gather the goal from the history by itself. The strongest evidence is Laban et al. (ICLR 2026): when a task arrives in pieces over several turns, the result falls by 39% on average across 15 models. If the same pieces are given in one message, the model keeps 95% of the result. The GOAL block is exactly such a "gluing of the pieces".

## In detail

**1. Dialogue state (DST, slot filling, task-oriented dialogue).** This is the classic "goal snapshot": a domain + slots, updated every turn; the metric is joint goal accuracy (the share of turns where all slots are right at once). Zero-shot through function calling (FnCTOD, 2024): GPT-4, 38.7% JGA on MultiWOZ 2.1; a self-correcting prompt (DST-as-SRP, 2024): 84.0% on 2.1 and 86.3% on 2.4 with a known domain, and 79.6% when the domain is predicted ([SRP](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.15861v2), [FnCTOD](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/large-language-models-as-zero-shot-dialogue-state-tracker-through-function-calling), [ReacTOD 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19077v1)). Lessons: (a) an explicit state works; (b) an error in the domain drags errors in the slots after it; (c) the update rules (add / replace / delete) are a difficulty of their own (ParsingDST, 2023). **For us:** our ontology is given by the form, and that is an advantage. "The domain" = the active goal, so the Jev question `goal_change` matters doubly: an error in it drags errors in everything else after it. For a change of goal, a high threshold, and when in doubt, an "A or B?" card.

**2. "Lost in a multi-turn conversation".** Laban et al. (Microsoft, Salesforce; ICLR 2026) gave a task in "shards", no more than one piece per turn. The result: −39% on average across 6 tasks and 15 models, including strong and reasoning models. The same pieces in one message give 95% of the result; the drop is visible from two turns already; the main cause is unreliability: early guesses get fixed ([arXiv 2505.06120](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.06120)). A 2026 work explains the drop by a mismatch of intent; its drop is about 30% ([arXiv 2602.07338](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.07338)). **For us:** this is the direct grounding of the GOAL block: the code glues the pieces into one place every turn. Plus [goal-from-pieces]: a guess does not become a fact and does not get fixed. For QA, a new kind of scenario, "the same task in one message and in pieces": the difference between the two variants is the snapshot's metric.

**3. User simulators and user modelling.** LLM simulators do not keep their own goal. UGST (a NeurIPS 2025 workshop) gives the simulator the goal's state before each answer, and wins noticeably on MultiWOZ 2.4 and τ-bench ([arXiv 2507.20152](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.20152)). "Drift No More?" (2025): drift from the goal reaches a plateau, and explicit reminders of the goal lower it ([arXiv 2510.07777](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.07777)). **For us:** the same as item 2, from the other side. For QA: a scripted guest with an explicit goal and its statuses gives an honest metric per subgoal, not only "task solved yes/no".

**4. Theory of mind in LLMs.** Successes on the classic false-belief tasks fall apart on small changes of the conditions (Ullman, 2023; "Clever Hans", 2023; Nickel et al., 2026: chain-of-thought sometimes even lowers accuracy) ([Ullman](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.08399), [Clever Hans](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.14763), [Nickel 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22072)). On tracking how a state changes over time, models lag behind people by 44.7% (Dynamic ToM, ACL 2025). In 2025–2026 benchmarks appeared for the gap between what the user thinks and what is really so (Beyond Words, 2026) and for the "belief, goal, action" cycle (UserHarness, 2026). **For us:** no free-form "describe what the person thinks". Jev gets narrow yes/no questions about the line, the code gets the state. One case of "the person thinks differently from how things are" is already covered by the rule [missing-fact].

**5. Plan and goal recognition.** The classic: the goal is the one for which the observed actions are most rational (Ramírez and Geffner, 2009/2010). With LLMs: CLIPS (AAMAS 2024), where the LLM scores the likelihood of lines and Bayesian inverse planning keeps a distribution over goals; this is better than GPT-4V. AutoToM (2025) does the same automatically ([CLIPS](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17930v1), [AutoToM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15676v1), [Gelpí 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.03682)). **For us:** I-1 is already built this way. What is missing is the upper level: an action is more likely if it serves the active goal. That is exactly step I-2: at first only a record in the trace, then a measurement.

**6. BDI agents (beliefs, desires, intentions).** Beliefs = what is known about the world; desires = goals; intentions = goals the agent has committed to. Cohen and Levesque's commitment rule (1990): an intention holds until the goal is reached, has become impossible or is no longer wanted. LLM versions of 2024–2026: NatBDI, ChatBDI, generating plans for AgentSpeak (ECAI 2025). The Utah 2026 report: an agent on a single LLM systematically violates the consistency of beliefs and commitment ([EMAS 2024](https://emas.in.tu-clausthal.de/2024/assets/papers/EMAS_2024_paper_13.pdf), [Utah 2026](https://www-old.cs.utah.edu/docs/techreports/2026/UUCS-26-001.pdf), [a 2025 review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.09458)). **For us:** the commitment needed is not the agent's but the person's: the goal's statuses active / done / dropped / blocked are exactly Cohen and Levesque's three conditions. They are the rules [settled-stays] (it holds), [can-stop] (impossible), [goal-changed] / [done-is-theirs] (not wanted, or reached). Only the person decides that the goal is no longer needed.

**7. Agent memory.** MemGPT / Letta: memory blocks always sit in the context and have a fixed character limit; the agent edits them with tools. There is no independent evaluation of the quality of such self-managed memory; the LoCoMo figure of 74% is from the authors themselves ([Letta docs](https://docs.letta.com/guides/agents/architectures/memgpt), [a review](https://zby.github.io/commonplace/agent-memory-systems/reviews/letta/)). LongMemEval (ICLR 2025): 500 questions on five abilities, including updating knowledge and abstaining from an answer. Long-context models lose 30–60%; adding extracted facts about the user to the search keys gives +4% to findability and +5% to the accuracy of answers ([LongMemEval](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.10813)). **For us:** GOAL is the same kind of block with a limit (up to 6 lines), but the code writes it from events, not the agent. The tool `note_goal`, that is self-editing by the agent, comes only after a measurement, because there are no independent data in its favour. Abstaining from an answer is, for us, open staying open.

**8. Task specifications and the "definition of done" for agents.** Kiro runs the work along the chain "requirements → design → tasks" and writes the acceptance criteria in the EARS notation ("When X, the system shall Y"). GitHub Spec Kit: a free-form spec.md → plan → tasks ([a review](https://www.morphllm.com/spec-driven-development), [a comparison](https://dev.to/rosgluk/github-spec-kit-vs-kiro-vs-claude-code-sdd-workflows-1fi1)). And our own OpenSpec is the same thing. The data in the sources are almost all vendors'; the R&D team does not trust the numbers. **For us:** the snapshot's "done" is acceptance criteria that the code checks: the required parts are filled, no limit is broken, the tool said "applied". From the same list [no-false-done] builds the final line for each goal. The agent does not report "done" while there is even one unmet criterion.

**9. Preference elicitation and active learning.** GATE (2023): the model asks about the task itself; STaR-GATE (2024): self-training of questions, 72% wins against the base model (the judge is GPT-4); OPEN (2024) chooses a question by the expected information; TO-GATE (AAAI 2025) ([STaR-GATE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.19154), [TO-GATE](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02827v1)). Questions pay off: Ambig-SWE, up to +74%; IN3, the agent finds >85% of vague tasks. But a person's attention is expensive: "Ask Less, Ask Better" (2026): a budget of 1–3 questions lowers the load on attention by 36–73% at 86–94% of the reward, across 8 models and 4 support domains ([the article](https://www.opentrain.ai/papers/ask-less-ask-better-allocating-user-attention-in-proactive-llm-agents-with-inter--arxiv-2610.24411/)). **For us:** the code counts the questions for each goal (the budget is set by the host's preset). An open part on which the next action does not depend is not asked about. Into the module, one line for [check-by-showing]: "ask only what changes the next action". RL training of questions is not taken: we have no data for it.

**10. Assistants in production.** ChatGPT's memory is shared across the account (since April 2024); what is saved can be viewed and deleted. Claude's memory (September–October 2025) is separate for each project, with viewing, editing and an incognito mode ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/memory), [Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/article/anthropic-brings-project-based-memory-to-paid-claude-users/)). Gemini's personal context is separate from the activity history. ChatGPT agent and Gemini's agent mode ask for confirmation before actions with consequences. Magentic-UI: a shared plan the person can edit. Alexa+ (February 2025) launched without some of the features shown. Apple postponed Siri's personal context: "harder than expected" ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/03/07/siri-apple-ai-delay), [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/08/09/alexa-plus-launch-bugs-delays-siri-comparison/)). **For us:** the best practice is memory bounded by the task (for us, the conversation thread), visible and editable. Shared memory between visits must be off by default; it is a separate business setting. A card "What I understood", with editing of each line, is our counterpart of "Manage memory"; the design team makes it. The lesson of Siri and Alexa: personal context for a wide audience is hard, so we start narrow: one thread, the task's snapshot, nothing personal.

**11. Proactive assistants and their failures.** ProactBench, ProAgentBench, Proactivity-Gym (2026) split the task into three questions: what the person needs, when to step in, and how exactly. There is no common evaluation standard; the main cost is interrupting ([ProAgentBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.04482.pdf), [LlamaPIE](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.04066v1)). **For us:** nothing new is taken. The core already limits proactivity: [when-then], one next step, [no-recommend-moments], [state-only-lowers]. The research confirms this is the right side. In the dashboard ([sec:long-goals]) the same budget applies: one question about a stalled goal, and no more reminders.

## What we integrate, in order

Ranked by the strength of the evidence and by the cost. Everything falls onto steps already proposed on [The goal module](../../how-it-works/goal-module/#35-the-order-of-work) §3.5; only three are new there: a budget of questions, a checkable "done" and the QA scenarios "in one message / in pieces".

1. **The GOAL block every turn** (items 2, 3, 7). The best proven: −39% without it against 95% with the gluing; drift goes down with reminders. M, already in the plan (steps 2 and 4).
2. **Slots with update operations, the active goal = the domain** (item 1). A high threshold for `goal_change`, because an error in the domain drags the rest after it. Within step 2.
3. **The goal's life cycle by BDI** (item 6): the statuses and the three conditions for the end of commitment are already in the module; they only need to be named the same way in the code. S.
4. **A budget of questions per goal and the rule "ask only what changes the action"** (item 9). New: a counter in code, the budget set by the host's preset, plus one line in [check-by-showing]. S. A scenario: on a form with 8 optional fields the agent asks no more than N questions.
5. **Hypotheses under the active goal (I-2)** (item 5). M, at first only the trace.
6. **A checkable "done"** (item 8): the code counts the goal's acceptance criteria, and the final line for each goal is built from them. S. A scenario: if a limit is broken, the agent does not say "done".
7. **A new kind of QA scenario: the pair "in one message / in pieces"** (item 2), and a metric per subgoal (item 3). It is the most direct figure for "how much the snapshot helps"; QA does it. S.
8. **A visible and editable snapshot** (item 10): the card "What I understood". M, by the design team, if wanted.

## What we do not take

- Memory across the whole account and between visits by default, as in ChatGPT. Ours are the visitors of someone else's business; only the business itself switches such memory on, with a separate setting.
- The agent's self-edited memory as the main source of the snapshot: there is no independent evaluation, and the form's events are more accurate.
- Free modelling of a person's beliefs in the spirit of ToM that goes beyond the screen and what was said: it breaks from small changes and leads to conclusions about the person.
- RL training of questions (STaR-GATE, TO-GATE): it needs data and training, and our model is behind an API. We can come back to it when the decision journal exists.
- Proactive hints beyond one next step.
- A full symbolic BDI engine or a planner: for a form and a site the statuses and rules are enough.
